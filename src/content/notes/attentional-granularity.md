A principle of the [Awareness layer](/notes/start-here), and the one this garden is built on. Depth is not a property of the content; it is a relationship between content and reader, where the right depth is whatever matches the reader's current attentional state.

The direction matters. Always gross to subtle, never the reverse. Starting with detail and asking the reader to summarize is overwhelm. Starting with the gist and letting the reader descend is honest.

**The design test**

> Does content depth track attentional depth, gross to subtle?

<details>
<summary>Show me what that means</summary>

You just used it. The summary above is the handle; this is the next layer down. A piece of writing can offer a sentence, a paragraph, or the whole essay, and let you choose how far to descend. Depth on demand, never depth imposed.

The full treatment is a ladder of seven granularities, from a two-word handle to a book, operationalized in the studio as the `semantic-zoom` discipline.

</details>

<details>
<summary>How it shows up in the work</summary>

- **Zenborg**: moments are one to three words, not paragraphs. Phases are coarser than clock times. The timeline shows three days, not a month. You zoom in as attention settles; the system never dumps a dashboard.
- **Respost**: envelope to glance, flap to signal intent, photo to commit, message for depth. You can stop at any layer with something complete.

Lineage and evidence: Cognitive Load Theory (Sweller), Information Foraging (Pirolli and Card), and the gross-to-subtle progression of Vipassana body-scanning.

</details>
