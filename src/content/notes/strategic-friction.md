The first principle of the [Equanimity layer](/notes/start-here). Not all friction is good. Bureaucratic friction is hostile. Strategic friction is compassionate: it slows automatic reactivity without blocking deliberate action. It tilts the easy default away from compulsion and toward intention.

**The design test**

> Is the compulsive path frictionless? If yes, it fails. Is the intentional path frictionless? If no, it fails.

<details>
<summary>How it shows up in the work</summary>

- **Zenborg**: the three-moments-per-phase constraint is friction by design. Adding a fourth requires deliberately removing another.
- **keel**: the intervention model is a tide driving a graduated friction dial, rendered gross to subtle: notice, then a granularity ceiling, then a delay or effort tax, then a single armed backstop. There are no walls. The hard block is kept for one defensible case only, the late-night backstop where the sleep-deprived self cannot self-assess.

Lineage: Hadash's Decoupling Model, and James Williams on adversarial design that privileges our impulses over our intentions.

</details>
