A principle of the [Equanimity layer](/notes/start-here), and the purest expression of the thesis. Who decides what fills your attention? An upstream allocator decides for you. A downstream tool leaves the deciding to you — and may propose what belongs in the slots, grounded in what you already told it you care about.

The distinction that matters is not "recommendations vs. no recommendations." It is: whose intentions drive the suggestion? A platform recommender serves engagement metrics. A downstream tool proposes from your declared habits, rhythms, and attitudes — and you dismiss or accept with zero consequence either way.

**The design test**

> Is every suggestion derived from what the user already told the system they care about? Can the user ignore it with zero consequence? Is anything auto-placed?

<details>
<summary>How it shows up in the work</summary>

- **Zenborg**: the system surfaces wilting habits, proposes cycle budgets, and suggests scheduling for gaps — all grounded in patterns the user already declared. The allocation decision always belongs to the person. The standing question is "where will I place my consciousness today?" The garden can whisper what might need tending.
- **Respost**: the sender fills the slots. No algorithm decides what fills the screen.

The image to hold: a recommender system is an upstream allocator that fills your slots before you arrive. A whiteboard with magnets is downstream — but a downstream tool can still hand you a magnet and say "this one hasn't been on the board in a while."

</details>
