A principle of the [Equanimity layer](/notes/start-here), and the purest expression of the thesis. Who decides what fills your attention? An upstream allocator, like a recommender, decides for you. A downstream tool leaves the deciding to you, and only surfaces context so you can decide well.

**The design test**

> Who decided what the user is looking at right now, the user or the system?

<details>
<summary>How it shows up in the work</summary>

- **Zenborg**: no feed, no recommendations, no auto-placement. The system surfaces context, whispers and dormancy indicators, but the allocation decision always belongs to the person. The standing question is "where will I place my consciousness today?"
- **Respost**: the sender fills the slots. No algorithm decides what fills the screen.

The image to hold: a recommender system is an upstream allocator that fills your slots before you arrive. A whiteboard with magnets is downstream. The slots are empty until you place them.

</details>
